# What is Top3?

Top3 is a social discovery platform built around one simple question:

> **What are your Top 3?**

A person's favourites reveal more than a generic like or follow.

Choosing three forces prioritization.

Ranking those three adds meaning.

Comparing those rankings creates context.

Top3 turns those choices into a way to express identity, discover entertainment, and find people whose taste meaningfully overlaps with your own.

**It is social discovery through taste.**

---

# The Product Idea

Top3 starts with Lists.

Not generic posts.

Not status updates.

Not endless content creation.

A user creates a ranked Top 3 List around something they care about.

That List can then contribute to:

- their profile;
- the Feed;
- Discover;
- community rankings;
- Overall rankings;
- Taste Match;
- recommendations;
- likes;
- comments;
- sharing;
- conversation.

The List is the basic unit of self-expression.

Everything social grows from it.

---

# Why Top3 Exists

Most social products begin with people.

You follow friends.

You watch creators.

You consume a stream.

Top3 begins with taste.

Instead of asking:

> “Who should I follow?”

Top3 asks:

> **“Who loves the same things I do?”**

Shared taste becomes the beginning of discovery.

A person does not need to know who someone is before becoming interested in what they love.

---

# The Problem

People constantly exchange recommendations:

> “What should I watch?”

> “What should I read?”

> “What should I listen to?”

> “What should I play?”

> “What podcast should I try?”

The answers are scattered across group chats, screenshots, Notes, social posts, streaming services, review platforms, and memory.

The bigger problem is not a lack of recommendations.

It is a lack of context.

A single recommendation tells you very little about the person giving it.

A ranked Top 3 tells you considerably more.

Top3 adds context by showing:

- what consistently rises to the top;
- how people rank similar favourites;
- where two people's taste overlaps;
- which people repeatedly recommend things that resonate.

---

# Our Vision

> **To become the easiest place in the world to discover people through shared taste.**

---

# Our Mission

Help people discover new favourites by first discovering people with tastes they trust.

---

# The Feeling We Want to Create

Top3 is not designed to maximize scrolling.

It is designed to create moments.

Moments where someone thinks:

> **“Wait…you love that too?”**

Those moments create curiosity.

Curiosity leads to exploration.

Exploration creates conversation.

Conversation can create connection.

Every List, Taste Match, shared pick, profile, recommendation, and community ranking should increase the chances of creating that moment.

When someone closes Top3, the ideal outcome is not simply:

> “I found a good movie.”

It is:

> **“I found my kind of people.”**

---

# Core Product Principles

## Taste First

Everything begins with taste.

Top3 should connect people through what they genuinely care about rather than through demographic assumptions or generic popularity.

Taste is expressed through choices.

Ranked choices make that taste more legible.

---

## Lists Before Posts

Top3 is built around thoughtfully curated Lists rather than generic social posts.

The product should encourage users to make meaningful choices instead of rewarding continuous low-friction content production.

The goal is not to create the largest possible volume of content.

The goal is to make each List worth looking at.

---

## Rankings Matter

A ranked List tells a story.

Choosing a favourite is useful.

Choosing a Top 3 is revealing.

Ranking those three makes the opinion more expressive.

Position matters.

A shared #1 can be more meaningful than simply having the same item somewhere in a List.

Top3 should preserve the meaning of rank wherever possible.

---

## Curation Over Volume

Three is a constraint by design.

The limitation creates clarity.

Users should feel that publishing a List means making a choice, not filling a database.

Top3 should resist product mechanics that dilute that intentionality.

---

## Explainable Discovery

Recommendations should feel understandable.

Whenever possible, Top3 should be able to show **why** a person, List, or recommendation is relevant.

Shared ranked picks are more valuable than opaque algorithmic similarity.

The product should prefer:

> “You both ranked these highly.”

over:

> “Recommended for you.”

without explanation.

---

## Shared Taste Creates Better Conversations

The most interesting interaction is often not agreement itself, but how people differ.

Top3 should encourage conversations such as:

> “I can't believe you ranked Arrival over Interstellar.”

> “That’s your #1?”

> “I forgot how good that was.”

> “I’ve never heard of this one.”

Conversation begins with something specific.

---

## Product Before Commitment

New users should experience the core product before being asked to invest heavily in setup.

That is why the V1 onboarding model lets a signed-out user build their first List before authentication.

Account creation happens when the user reaches Publish and has something worth saving.

The principle is broader than onboarding:

> **Demonstrate value before asking for commitment.**

---

## Community Without Popularity Pressure

Top3 should help users find people whose taste is useful to them.

Following is not primarily about popularity.

The most valuable account may be someone with a small audience whose recommendations consistently resonate.

The product should avoid turning follower count into the central measure of worth.

---

## Privacy is Part of Discovery

Discovery must respect account visibility.

Private users should not be surfaced in ways that undermine the reason they chose to be private.

Social discovery only works if users trust the product.

---

## Simplicity Wins

Creating a List should feel easy.

Browsing should feel easy.

Understanding a recommendation should feel easy.

The interface should not compete with the content.

When two solutions provide the same value, prefer the simpler one.

---

## Content Before Chrome

The ranked items are the visual focus.

Interface design exists to make content easier to understand, compare, and act on.

If a design decision makes the List harder to scan, it is probably the wrong decision.

---

# Lists and Overall

Top3 contains two complementary expressions of taste.

## Personal Lists

A List says:

> **“These are my Top 3.”**

It represents an individual's taste.

## Overall Rankings

Overall says:

> **“These are our Top 3.”**

It represents the aggregate signal created by published community Lists.

Together, the two concepts show how individual opinion contributes to collective taste.

That relationship is central enough to Top3 that it is introduced during first-run onboarding.

---

# Taste Match

Taste Match is the clearest expression of Top3's social premise.

It asks:

> **“How much meaningful ranked taste do we share?”**

Taste Match should not be treated as a generic compatibility score.

Its value comes from the shared ranked picks underneath it.

Users should be able to understand the evidence behind the match.

The percentage creates curiosity.

The shared picks create credibility.

The differences create conversation.

---

# Current V1 Category Focus

Top3's long-term concept can extend to many forms of taste.

The current V1 product deliberately focuses on entertainment and media categories:

- Albums
- Artists
- Books
- Movies
- Podcasts
- Songs
- TV Shows
- Video Games

This narrower launch scope lets the product establish its core social and ranking behaviour before expanding into additional domains.

Potential future categories may include areas such as:

- restaurants;
- travel;
- outdoor destinations;
- food;
- games beyond video games;
- other recommendation-rich interests.

Category expansion should happen when the new domain strengthens the same core behaviour rather than simply increasing breadth.

---

# Who Top3 Is For

Top3 is for people who:

- love sharing recommendations;
- enjoy comparing opinions;
- remember their favourites;
- like discovering new entertainment;
- care about why someone recommends something;
- believe their favourite things say something about who they are;
- enjoy finding people whose taste they trust.

Top3 is built for curiosity.

---

# What Makes Top3 Different

Top3 is not trying to replace general-purpose social networks.

Those platforms are often built around questions such as:

> “What’s happening?”

> “What are you doing?”

> “What are people watching right now?”

Top3 asks:

> **“What do you love most?”**

That difference matters.

Top3 is less interested in what is momentarily visible and more interested in what consistently matters to someone.

# The Core User Loop

## Create

Make a ranked Top 3 List.

Express an opinion clearly.

---

## Contribute

Publishing adds the List to the community.

That List can contribute to community and Overall ranking signals.

---

## Discover

Explore:

- Lists;
- categories;
- topics and genres;
- Overall rankings;
- people;
- recommendations;
- similar taste.

---

## Connect

Follow people whose recommendations consistently resonate.

For private accounts, respect the follow-request boundary.

---

## Compare

Use Taste Match and shared ranked picks to understand where tastes overlap.

Notice the differences as well as the agreements.

---

## Participate

Like.

Comment.

Publish more Lists.

Share something worth discussing.

---

## Return

Come back because there is something meaningful to discover:

- a new List;
- a new recommendation;
- a new match;
- a new conversation;
- a new ranking;
- a new interest worth exploring.

---

# Sharing

Top3 content should be worth sending to another person.

A strong List creates the impulse:

> “You need to see this.”

A surprising Overall ranking creates the impulse:

> “Can you believe this is the community Top 3?”

Sharing should extend discovery beyond the app without becoming the core engagement mechanic.

The long-term product should make shared content understandable even before the recipient becomes a Top3 user.

---

# Recommendations

Top3 recommendations should become more useful as the taste graph becomes richer.

The goal is not to predict everything a person will like.

The goal is to make discovery more relevant by identifying people and Lists with meaningful taste overlap.

Good recommendation systems should:

- use ranked preference as signal;
- remain explainable;
- preserve serendipity;
- avoid reducing discovery to popularity;
- help users find people, not just items.

AI may eventually assist recommendation and discovery, but it should strengthen human curation rather than replace it.

---

# Design Philosophy

The interface should feel calm.

Structure should do more work than decoration.

Whitespace is intentional.

Cards are clean.

Typography is clear.

Motion supports understanding.

The user's Lists and ranked items remain the visual focus.

Light and dark appearance should feel like the same product.

Preview experiences may intentionally create a more distinct media layer, but the application itself should remain coherent and quiet.

The design should disappear so taste can take centre stage.

---

# Product Decision Filter

When faced with two design options:

Choose the clearer one.

When faced with two interaction models:

Choose the one that preserves understanding with less friction.

When deciding whether to build a feature, ask:

> **Does this help people express taste, discover through taste, or connect through shared taste?**

If the answer is no, question whether the feature belongs.

When deciding whether to add engagement mechanics, also ask:

> **Does this make the experience more meaningful, or merely more addictive?**

Top3 should optimize for usefulness and connection rather than time spent.

# Success Looks Like

A user opens Top3 looking for something interesting.

They leave having discovered:

- a Movie;
- a Book;
- a Song;
- an Album;
- an Artist;
- a TV Show;
- a Video Game;
- a Podcast;
- or someone worth following.

The greatest success is not simply finding one recommendation.

It is finding a person whose recommendations repeatedly matter.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Top3 can become a rich graph of human preferences.

Not simply what people like.

But:

- what they rank;
- how strongly they rank it;
- where those rankings overlap;
- which people's recommendations prove useful over time;
- how individual preferences combine into community taste.

The goal is not to collect the most content.

The goal is to make taste legible.

Because once taste becomes legible:

recommendations can become more personal;

communities can become more meaningful;

discovery can become more human.

---

# Our North Star

Top3 is not really about three-item Lists.

The Lists are the language people use to introduce themselves.

Every feature should make it easier to answer one question:

> **“Who out there sees the world the way I do?”**

If Top3 makes answering that question easier, the product is moving in the right direction.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every product decision should reinforce one belief:

> **Shared taste is the beginning of meaningful connection.**

If a feature strengthens that belief, it belongs in Top3.

If it does not, we should question whether it belongs at all.
