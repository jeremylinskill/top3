# What is Top3?

Top3 is a social platform built around one simple question:

> **"What are your Top 3?"**

Whether it's movies, books, bands, restaurants, podcasts, ski resorts, hiking trails, or coffee shops, everyone has favorites. Those favorites say something about who we are.

Top3 helps people share those preferences, discover others with similar tastes, and start meaningful conversations through ranked lists instead of endless photos, opinions, or algorithms.

**It's social discovery through taste.**

---

# Why Top3 Exists

Most social platforms are built around people.

You follow friends.

You watch influencers.

You consume content.

Top3 is different.

It starts with interests.

Rather than asking:

> "Who should I follow?"

Top3 asks:

> **"Who loves the same things I do?"**

Shared taste becomes the beginning of every relationship.

---

# The Problem

Today our favorite things are scattered everywhere.

Movies live on Letterboxd.

Books live on Goodreads.

Music lives on Spotify.

Restaurants live on Google Maps.

Travel recommendations live in notes apps.

Group chats.

Instagram stories.

Screenshots.

People constantly ask:

> "What should I watch?"

> "What should I read?"

> "What should I eat?"

> "Where should I go?"

Everyone has recommendations.

Almost nobody has context.

Top3 provides that context.

Instead of recommending one thing...

…it shows what consistently rises to the top of someone's rankings.

---

# Our Vision

**To become the easiest place in the world to discover people through shared taste.**

---

# Our Mission

Help people discover new favorites by first discovering people with similar tastes.

---

# The Feeling We Want to Create

Top3 isn't designed to maximize scrolling.

It's designed to create moments.

Moments where someone thinks:

> **"Wait...you love that too?"**

Those moments create curiosity.

Curiosity leads to conversation.

Conversation builds connection.

Every recommendation, every Taste Match, every profile, and every shared favorite should increase the chances of creating that moment.

When people close Top3, we don't want them thinking:

> "I found a good movie."

We want them thinking:

> **"I found my kind of people."**

---

# Core Principles

## Taste First

Everything begins with taste.

People don't connect because they share demographics.

They connect because they love the same things.

---

## Simplicity Wins

Creating a Top3 should feel effortless.

Browsing should feel effortless.

The interface should never compete with the content.

---

## Content Before Chrome

The ranked items are the product.

The interface exists to support discovery—not distract from it.

If a design decision makes the content harder to scan, it's the wrong decision.

---

## Rankings Matter

A ranked list tells a story.

Choosing a favorite is interesting.

Choosing your Top 3 is revealing.

Position matters.

Shared #1 picks matter even more.

---

## Shared Taste Creates Better Conversations

Instead of generic comments like:

> "Nice list."

Top3 encourages conversations like:

> "I can't believe you ranked Arrival over Interstellar."

> "I forgot how good this book was."

> "I've never heard of that band."

Conversation begins with shared interests.

---

# Who Top3 Is For

Top3 is for people who:

- Love sharing recommendations.
- Love discovering new things.
- Enjoy comparing opinions.
- Believe their favorite things say something about who they are.

Top3 is built for curiosity.

---

# What Makes Top3 Different

Top3 is not another social network.

It isn't trying to replace Instagram.

It isn't trying to replace TikTok.

It isn't trying to replace Reddit.

Instead, it complements them.

Those platforms answer:

> **"What's happening?"**

Top3 answers:

> **"What do you love?"**

They leave having discovered:

- A movie.
- A book.
- A destination.
- A restaurant.
- Or someone worth following.

The greatest success isn't simply finding a recommendation.

It's finding someone whose recommendations consistently matter.

---

# Design Philosophy

The interface should feel calm.

Whitespace is intentional.

Cards are clean.

Typography is strong.

The user's rankings should always be the visual focus.

Every interaction should feel lightweight.

Nothing should feel noisy.

The design should quietly disappear so taste can take center stage.

---

# Product Principles

When faced with two design options:

Choose the simpler one.

When faced with two interaction models:

Choose the one requiring fewer taps.

When deciding whether to build a new feature, ask:

> **Does this help people discover others through shared taste?**

If the answer is no...

don't build it.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Top3 becomes the world's largest graph of human preferences.

Not just what people like...

…but how strongly they like it.

The goal isn't to collect content.

The goal is to understand taste.

Because once taste is understood...

recommendations become personal.

Communities become meaningful.

Discovery becomes effortless.

---

# Our North Star

Top3 isn't really about Top 3 lists.

The lists are simply the language people use to introduce themselves.

Every feature we build should help someone answer one question:

> **"Who out there sees the world the way I do?"**

If we make answering that question easier, we're building the right product.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every product decision should reinforce one simple belief:

**Shared taste is the beginning of meaningful connection.**

If a feature strengthens that belief, it belongs in Top3.

If it doesn't, we should question whether it belongs at all.
